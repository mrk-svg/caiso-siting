# Node Watch #1 — what CAISO's own files say about where to interconnect

*Draft for sending. Every number is from the CAISO Public Queue Report (run date 2026-09-14), the Cluster 15
report (posted 2026-07-16), the 2024/2025 TPD allocation results, and CAISO's 2026-01-15 notice on PG&E POI
availability. Nothing below states a cause; the files carry none. Source rows and methods:
https://mrk-svg.github.io/caiso-siting/*

**Subject:** Node Watch #1 — Cluster 15 has shed 52%; here is where the survivors are

Hi —

I built a free tool that reads CAISO's queue files every Monday and lays them out per point of interconnection:
what is queued, what is operating, what withdrew since 2022 and whether it was storage, what the 2025 TPD cycle
actually allocated, and which POIs the utility has said in writing are available for Cluster 16. Six things from
the first run that I think are worth two minutes of a siting person's time.

**1. Cluster 15 has lost 52% of the MW that entered it.** 84 of 170 requests, 30,564 MW of 59,013, have withdrawn;
86 projects and 28,449 MW remain (21,513 MW of it storage). The exits came in two waves: April–May 2025
(25 projects, 11,087 MW) and November–December 2025 (37 projects, 10,680 MW), with a smaller one in June–July 2026
(22 projects, 8,796 MW).

**2. The 2025 TPD cycle refused more than it granted.** Across the file, 28,151 MW was requested and 11,530 MW
allocated; 82 rows received exactly 0%. At the node level the outright refusals are concentrated: Colorado River–Palo
Verde 1,000 MW denied, East County 973, Colorado River Substation 875, Imperial Valley 777, Trout Canyon 625, Wheeler
Ridge 600 — all at 0%. Two nodes got everything they asked for: Moss Landing (1,500 MW) and Rio Hondo (600 MW). Gates
asked for 1,396 MW and received 396. If you are counting on FCDS at a Southern California or Nevada 500 kV node, the
2025 result is the base rate.

**3. Three PG&E switching stations are officially "available" for Cluster 16, and one of them is already crowded.**
CAISO's January notice names Dry Lake, Harlan and Bitterwater as new 500/230 kV stations viable for C16, Tesla as
conditional, and Gates as relieved of its space limitation. Dry Lake already carries 2,950 MW of active Cluster 15
requests (2,050 MW of it seeking full capacity) and has had zero MW withdraw since 2022. Harlan has 1,450 MW queued;
Bitterwater 300 MW. Available is not the same as empty.

**4. All-time churn lies about storage nodes; the 2022-onward window does not.** Whirlwind (Kern, SCE) has an
all-time withdrawn-to-surviving ratio of 1.32 — a graveyard on paper. Restrict to storage withdrawn since 2022 and it
is 0.09, with 2,120 MW operating and 3,273 MW in pipeline; the wreckage is 2008-era wind. The opposite case is Lugo:
4,953 MW withdrawn since 2022 against 2,298 MW of storage still queued (churn 2.16), and only 29% of its Cluster 15 MW
survives. Red Bluff sits between: 3,452 MW left *after* Phase II results, i.e. with upgrade costs in hand.

**5. Cluster 14 is holding far better than Cluster 13 did at the same age.** Kaplan–Meier on withdrawal: at month 12,
94% of C14 projects were still in the queue versus 51% of C13. Clusters 10–13 all reached median time-to-withdrawal
at 12–13 months; C14's median is 44 months. Cluster 15, 17 months in, sits at 64% — between the two regimes.
Completion is treated as success, not an event.

**6. Cluster 15 nodes with a large surviving request and under 300 MW withdrawn since 2022:** Dry Lake (2,950 MW
active, 0 withdrawn), Los Banos–Gates #1 (800 / 0), Hassayampa (700 / 0), Magunden–Pastoria (600 / 0), Lugo–Pisgah
(700 / 150), Calcite (638 / 200), Quinto (500 / 100). Not a recommendation — a list of places where the file shows
requests entering and not leaving.

The caveats, out loud: POI strings are free text, so a node is my normalisation of CAISO's spelling and the map
positions are OpenStreetMap matches with a confidence score you can inspect. Cluster 15 "deliverability" is
requested, not allocated. Withdrawal is not failure at the node and CAISO gives no reason beyond "IC request".
None of this is load-side; data-center interconnection has no public queue.
